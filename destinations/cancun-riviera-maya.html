Overview

Why families choose Cancún & Riviera Maya.

The Riviera Maya is a long stretch of Caribbean coastline running south from Cancún past Puerto Morelos, Playa del Carmen and Akumal down to Tulum. It is one of the easiest places to plan a family beach vacation: direct flights from many U.S. and Canadian cities, a huge range of all-inclusive resorts, and plenty to do beyond the pool.

Families love the mix. You can spend mornings on a calm, shallow beach, then spend an afternoon floating through a cenote (a freshwater sinkhole), exploring a Maya archaeological site, or snorkeling with sea turtles. Resorts here range from lively, kid-centered properties with water parks and kids' clubs to quiet, design-forward hideaways, so matching the resort to your family matters a great deal.

Best for

Dry, sunny and comfortable from about mid-December through April. Summer is hotter and more humid, with afternoon showers. Hurricane season runs June through November, with the highest risk typically in late summer and early fall, so travel insurance is strongly worth considering then. Seaweed (sargassum) can affect some Caribbean-facing beaches, most often in spring and summer, and it varies from year to year and beach to beach. We check recent conditions before recommending a resort.

Getting There & Around

Making the journey simple.

By air

Cancún International (CUN) is the main gateway, with nonstop service from many U.S. and Canadian cities. Playa del Carmen and Tulum are reached by ground transfer from the same airport.

Transfers

Expect roughly 20 to 30 minutes to the Cancún Hotel Zone, about 45 minutes to an hour to Playa del Carmen, and up to around 1.5 hours to Tulum, depending on traffic. We arrange private or shared transfers so there is no negotiating at the curb.

Getting around

Most families rely on resort shuttles, private drivers and organized tours. Taxis are plentiful in town areas but agree on the fare first. Renting a car is possible, but many families find tours and drivers simpler.

Ferries

Passenger ferries connect Playa del Carmen to Cozumel and Cancún's Puerto Juárez area to Isla Mujeres, both easy day trips.

Where to Stay

Choosing the right area.

Cancún Hotel Zone

Long beach strip with big resorts, restaurants, shopping and nightlife close by. Easy and convenient, especially for shorter trips. Some stretches have calmer water than others.

Costa Mujeres & Puerto Morelos

Quieter, newer resort areas north and south of Cancún. Good for families who want space and calmer surroundings.

Playa del Carmen

A walkable town with the lively Quinta Avenida, restaurants and easy ferry access to Cozumel. Great for families with teens who like to wander.

Riviera Maya all-inclusive corridor

Large, family-oriented resorts between Playa del Carmen and Akumal, many with kids' clubs, water parks and multiple restaurants.

Akumal & Tulum

Laid-back, nature-focused, with turtles, cenotes and the cliffside Tulum ruins. Best for families who prefer boutique and eco-style stays.

Things to Do

Experiences families love.

Cenotes

Swim in clear freshwater sinkholes. Some are open-air and shallow, others are caves. We match the cenote to your children's ages and comfort in the water.

Maya ruins

Tulum sits on a cliff above the sea; Chichén Itzá is a long day trip; Cobá lets you climb with a guide in some areas (access rules change, so we confirm).

Eco-adventure parks

Xcaret, Xel-Há and Xplor combine snorkeling, rivers, zip lines and shows. These are big days, so plan rest around them.

Snorkel & dive

Reef snorkeling, swimming with sea turtles in Akumal, and Cozumel's famed reefs for older kids and teens.

Isla Mujeres

A relaxed island day trip with golf-cart rentals, calm beaches and a sculpture park underwater for snorkelers.

Seasonal wildlife

Whale shark excursions are typically offered in summer months, and sea turtle nesting happens in warmer months, with strict rules to protect the animals.

Good to Know

Practical details before you go.

Entry

U.S. and Canadian citizens need a valid passport and a Mexican tourist permit, which is usually handled digitally or by the airline. Requirements can change, and we confirm them at booking.

Money

Pesos are used locally, but USD is widely accepted at resorts and tourist shops. Carry small bills for tips.

Water & food

Drink bottled or purified water. Resorts serve purified water and ice.

Sun & reef

Use biodegradable sunscreen where required, especially at cenotes and eco parks, and bring more sun protection than you think you need.

Safety

Resort and tourist areas are heavily visited by families. Use licensed transport, stay aware as you would in any large destination, and check the current U.S. and Canadian travel advisories before you go.

Fees

A visitor fee may apply in Quintana Roo, often collected through your airline or tour operator. We note this in your trip details.

Planning Tips

How we'd plan it.

Advisor tips

  • Pick the resort for your kids' ages. A great toddler resort and a great teen resort are often different properties.
  • Book popular park days and tours before you arrive. They can sell out in peak weeks.
  • Build a rest day into the middle of the week. Sun and water wear out kids fast.
  • Consider a swim-up or ground-floor room for families with young children.

Questions

Common questions.

Is the Riviera Maya good for toddlers?

Yes, many resorts have shallow splash areas, kids' clubs and relaxed dining. Look for calm beach entry, shade and rooms close to the main areas.

How many days do we need?

Most families enjoy 5 to 7 nights. That allows time for the beach plus two or three excursions without feeling rushed.

Do we need a passport for every child?

Yes. Every traveler needs a valid passport. Check expiry dates early, since passports can take weeks to renew.

Is it better to stay in Cancún or Playa del Carmen?

Cancún is convenient and resort-focused. Playa del Carmen offers a walkable town. We help you choose based on how your family likes to spend its evenings.

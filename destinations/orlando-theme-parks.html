Overview

Why families choose Orlando Theme Parks.

Orlando is home to some of the world's most popular theme parks, including multiple Walt Disney World parks, the Universal Orlando Resort parks and SeaWorld Orlando. Planning matters here: park tickets, hotel location, dining reservations and line-skipping options all affect how smoothly your trip goes.

A good Orlando trip is built around your family's ages and energy. Toddlers and preschoolers have different needs than teens who want thrill rides, and a smart plan includes pool days and breaks. We help you choose the right parks, the right length of trip and the right hotel.

Best for

  • Families with young children who love characters and stories
  • Families with teens who want big rides
  • Multigenerational groups, with plenty of rest areas
  • Families who want a drivable or short-flight vacation
  • Celebrations such as birthdays and milestones

Crowds and weather both shape the best times. January (after the first week), early May, and September through mid-November tend to be quieter. Spring break, Easter, summer, Thanksgiving week and the Christmas holidays are the busiest. Summer is hot and humid with daily afternoon thunderstorms, and Florida's hurricane season runs June through November.

Getting There & Around

Making the journey simple.

By air

Orlando International (MCO) is a major hub with nonstop flights from most U.S. and many Canadian cities.

Transfers

Most theme-park hotels are 20 to 45 minutes from the airport. We arrange transfers, or you can use a rental car or ride-share.

Getting around

Disney resort hotels have their own transport. Others rely on shuttles, ride-share or rental cars. Parking fees and driving times add up, so we weigh the options.

Other attractions

Kennedy Space Center and Florida's beaches can be added for variety.

Where to Stay

Choosing the right area.

On-site resort hotels

Staying at a Disney or Universal hotel can offer convenient transport and perks such as early entry. Perks change, so we confirm what is current.

Good to Know

Practical details before you go.

Ticketing

Ticket options, line-skipping systems and reservation windows change often. We explain what is current and what suits your family.

Height rules

Many rides have minimum height requirements. Check these when planning for younger children.

Heat & hydration

Florida's heat is intense in summer. Plan breaks, shade and water.

Strollers & accessibility

Parks offer stroller rentals and accessibility services. Tell us your needs and we plan around them.

Dining

Popular restaurants fill up, and reservations may open weeks or months in advance.

Planning Tips

How we'd plan it.

Advisor tips

  • Plan a rest day or half day for every two park days.
  • Arrive at park opening, and plan the afternoon around pool time.
  • Match ride choices to the shortest rider in your group.
  • Book dining and special experiences as soon as reservation windows open.

Questions

Common questions.

How many park days do we need?

Most families do well with three to five park days, plus one or two rest days.

What age is best for Orlando?

Ages three to ten are a sweet spot for characters and stories, but teens enjoy the thrill parks. We plan around your family.

Should we stay on property?

It depends on your priorities. On-site hotels offer convenience and sometimes perks, while off-site can offer more space or value.

When are the lowest crowds?

Typically January after the first week, early May, and September to mid-November, outside holidays.

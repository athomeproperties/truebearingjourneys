Overview

Why families choose Mediterranean Cruises.

A Mediterranean cruise lets you sample several countries without repacking or arranging trains and hotels. Itineraries commonly visit ports in Italy, Greece, Spain, France and Croatia, putting you within reach of the Colosseum, the Acropolis, the Amalfi Coast and island villages with white-washed houses.

It works well for families with older kids and teens, and for multigenerational groups, because the ship is a comfortable base and everyone can choose their own pace in port. Because port days can be short and sights can be crowded, a bit of planning for each port pays off.

Best for

  • Families with school-age kids and teens
  • Multigenerational groups
  • Culture, history and food lovers
  • First-time visitors to Europe
  • Families who want to see several places in one trip

Most Mediterranean cruises run from about April through October, with some year-round sailings in the western Mediterranean. May, June, September and October are usually the best balance of weather and crowds. July and August are hot and busy, with the most crowded ports. Winter sailings are limited.

Getting There & Around

Making the journey simple.

Where ships depart

Barcelona, Rome (Civitavecchia), Venice, Athens (Piraeus) and other ports start and end many itineraries.

Plan to arrive early

We suggest arriving one to two days before your sailing so a flight delay does not cost you the ship, and you can adjust to time changes.

Port logistics

Some ports are well outside the city they serve. For example, Rome's cruise port is a significant ride from the city, so we help plan timing.

Add a stay

Many families add a few nights in Rome, Barcelona or Athens before or after the cruise.

Where to Stay

Choosing the right area.

Western Mediterranean

Spain, France and Italy, including cities like Barcelona, Marseille, Rome and Naples, with access to Pompeii and the Amalfi Coast.

Eastern Mediterranean

Greece and the Greek islands such as Santorini and Mykonos, plus Athens.

Adriatic

Venice and Croatia's walled city of Dubrovnik, a scenic and history-rich route.

Ship size

Larger ships have more family features, while smaller ships can reach more intimate ports. We match size to your family.

Good to Know

Practical details before you go.

Documents

A valid passport is required. Europe is rolling out new entry systems, including a digital entry and exit system and a planned travel authorization for visitors, so we confirm what applies to your sailing.

Heat & crowds

Summer is hot, with long lines. Start early and take midday breaks.

Walking

Expect cobblestones, stairs and steep climbs. Comfortable shoes matter.

Dress codes

Churches and some sites require covered shoulders and knees.

Pickpockets

Crowded tourist spots attract pickpockets. Use a cross-body bag and stay alert.

Timing

Pay attention to all-aboard times. Ship excursions are generally held for you if delayed, while independent tours are not.

Planning Tips

How we'd plan it.

Advisor tips

  • Choose a few must-see sights per port and skip the rest.
  • Book popular timed-entry attractions early.
  • Build in a day before the cruise for jet lag.
  • Mix port days with sea days so everyone can rest.

Questions

Common questions.

Is a Mediterranean cruise good for kids?

Yes, especially for school-age children and teens. Many ships have kids' clubs, and ports offer history, beaches and gelato.

What is the best time to go?

May, June, September and October for balanced weather and crowds.

Do we need a passport?

Yes. We also confirm any additional entry requirements at booking.

Ship excursions or independent?

Ship excursions are simpler and protect you from missing the ship. Independent exploring offers flexibility with more planning.

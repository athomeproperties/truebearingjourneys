Overview

Why families choose Alaska Cruises.

An Alaska cruise is one of the best ways to see a place that is hard to reach any other way. Ships glide through the Inside Passage and into fjords, past towering glaciers and forested islands, with chances to spot humpback whales, orcas, sea otters, bald eagles and, on excursions, brown bears.

It is a great multigenerational trip because everyone can choose their own pace: grandparents can watch the scenery from a balcony while teens try a zip line or dog-sled excursion. Most itineraries are 7 nights, and many families add a few days on land to see Denali or Anchorage.

Best for

  • Multigenerational families
  • Nature and wildlife lovers
  • Families who want a cruise but not a beach
  • Active families who enjoy excursions
  • Travelers who like scenery and fresh air

Most Alaska cruises sail from about May through September. May and June are typically drier, with long days and fewer crowds. July and August are the warmest and the busiest, with good wildlife viewing. September is cooler and rainier, with lower demand and fall color starting. Weather can change fast, so layers and rain gear are essential in any month.

Getting There & Around

Making the journey simple.

Round-trip cruises

Seattle and Vancouver are the most common starting points for round-trip Inside Passage cruises, with some sailings from other West Coast ports.

One-way cruises

Gulf of Alaska itineraries typically run one way between Vancouver and Seward or Whittier (near Anchorage) and visit glaciers like Hubbard or College Fjord.

Cruise & land tours

Many families add a land tour with Denali National Park, Anchorage and scenic rail journeys before or after the cruise.

Getting to the ship

Fly into the departure city a day early so a delayed flight does not mean a missed ship.

Where to Stay

Choosing the right area.

Inside Passage cruise

A scenic loop with ports like Juneau, Ketchikan, Skagway and Sitka, and glacier viewing in places such as Tracy Arm or Glacier Bay.

Gulf of Alaska cruise

A one-way route that often includes Hubbard Glacier, with the option to add a land tour.

Cabin choices

A balcony gives you private views on scenic days, and many families consider it worth the upgrade. We help you match the cabin to your group.

Ship style

From large family-focused ships with kids' clubs to smaller, expedition-style vessels. We help match ship, style and age range.

Good to Know

Practical details before you go.

Documents

A passport is strongly recommended for every traveler. Itineraries often include Canadian ports, so we confirm the exact document requirements for your sailing.

Packing

Pack layers: a waterproof jacket, warm hat and gloves, comfortable shoes and sunglasses.

Excursions

Popular excursions can sell out, so we help you book early.

Seasickness

Inside Passage waters are usually calm, but Gulf of Alaska crossings can be rougher. Pack remedies if anyone is sensitive.

Connectivity

Internet is limited in remote areas, and some ports have little or no cell coverage.

Accessibility

Tell us about mobility needs. Some ports use tenders or involve uneven ground.

Planning Tips

How we'd plan it.

Advisor tips

  • Book early for the best cabin choices on your preferred dates.
  • Consider a balcony for scenic days, or a smaller ship if you want a more intimate experience.
  • Pick two or three excursions that matter, and leave other ports for exploring.
  • Bring binoculars and a camera with a zoom lens.

Questions

Common questions.

Is an Alaska cruise good for kids?

Yes. Many ships have junior ranger-style programs and kids' clubs, and wildlife and glacier sightings are memorable at any age.

What is the best month to go?

May and June for fewer crowds and drier weather, July and August for the warmest days, and September for lower demand.

Do we need a passport?

We strongly recommend it, and some itineraries require it. We confirm requirements at booking.

Will we see the Northern Lights?

Not typically. Summer nights are too bright. Alaska cruises focus on glaciers and wildlife.

Overview

Why families choose Hawaii.

Hawaii is a state, not just a destination. That means U.S. citizens do not need a passport to visit, there is no currency exchange, and it feels familiar while looking like nowhere else. Each island has its own character, and choosing the right one for your family is the most important decision you will make.

Oahu is the energetic hub, with Waikiki, Honolulu and Pearl Harbor. Maui blends beaches, resorts and the Road to Hana. Kauai is lush and quiet, with dramatic cliffs and hiking. The Big Island has active volcanoes, black-sand beaches and stargazing. Many families pair two islands for a longer trip.

Best for

  • Families who want variety: beaches, hiking, culture and wildlife
  • Multigenerational trips (no passport needed for U.S. citizens)
  • Active families who like hiking, snorkeling and surfing lessons
  • History-minded families (Pearl Harbor and Hawaiian culture)
  • Longer vacations of a week or more

Hawaii is a year-round destination with warm temperatures. Winter (December to March) brings bigger surf on north shores, more rain on windward sides, and whale watching. Summer is calmer and drier. April to June and September to October are often the sweet spot, with pleasant weather and somewhat fewer crowds. Hurricane season officially runs June through November, though direct hits are uncommon. Holiday periods and summer are the busiest and most expensive.

Getting There & Around

Making the journey simple.

By air

Nonstop flights reach Honolulu, Maui, Kauai and the Big Island from many U.S. cities, and some Canadian cities. Flights from the East Coast are long, so plan for an overnight arrival or a recovery day.

Between islands

Short inter-island flights connect the islands. Plan on a half day when changing islands.

Getting around

Most islands require a rental car, with Waikiki being the notable exception where walking and ride-share are common. We help with timing, since rental cars can sell out.

Time zone

Hawaii does not observe daylight saving time, so the time difference with the mainland changes during the year.

Where to Stay

Choosing the right area.

Oahu: Waikiki & Honolulu

Big-city energy with beaches, shops, restaurants and easy access to Pearl Harbor and Diamond Head. Very convenient for families.

Maui: Wailea & Kaanapali

Resort areas with beautiful beaches and a polished feel. West Maui is still recovering from the 2023 Lahaina wildfire, and we recommend traveling with sensitivity and checking current conditions.

Kauai: Poipu & Princeville

Quieter and lush. South shore is sunnier, north shore is wetter and more dramatic.

Big Island: Kohala & Kona

Dry, sunny resort coasts, with volcanoes, stargazing and manta ray snorkeling nearby.

Good to Know

Practical details before you go.

Entry

U.S. citizens do not need a passport for domestic travel, but adults need acceptable ID for flights (such as REAL ID). Canadian and other international visitors need a passport. We confirm requirements at booking.

Reef-safe sunscreen

Hawaii restricts sunscreens containing certain chemicals that harm coral. Look for mineral-based, reef-safe options.

Ocean safety

Hawaii's ocean can be powerful. Check conditions, swim near lifeguards when possible, and never turn your back on the surf.

Nature & respect

Stay on marked trails and respect closures and cultural sites. Do not take lava rock or sand.

Costs

Hawaii is generally more expensive than the mainland, and resort fees are common. We include them in planning.

Reservations

Some popular sites require advance reservations. We note the ones relevant to your itinerary.

Planning Tips

How we'd plan it.

Advisor tips

  • Start with the right island, not the right resort.
  • Consider two islands for 8 or more nights, and avoid switching more than once.
  • Book rental cars early and confirm car seat needs.
  • Plan one big activity per day and leave space for beach time.

Questions

Common questions.

Which island is best for kids?

Oahu is easiest and the most varied, Maui is a classic resort island, Kauai is lush and calm, and the Big Island is great for volcano-curious families.

Do we need a passport?

U.S. citizens do not for domestic travel, but Canadian travelers do. Valid ID is needed for flights.

How many days do we need?

A week per island is comfortable, and 10 to 14 days allows two islands.

When are the whales here?

Humpback whales are typically seen from around December through April.

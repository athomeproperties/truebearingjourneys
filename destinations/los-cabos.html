Overview

Why families choose Los Cabos.

Los Cabos sits at the southern tip of Mexico's Baja California peninsula, where the Pacific Ocean meets the Sea of Cortez. The region includes lively Cabo San Lucas, the quieter and more traditional San José del Cabo, and the Tourist Corridor between them, lined with upscale resorts and golf courses.

The scenery is the star: golden desert against bright blue sea, rock formations including the famous Arch at Land's End, and in winter, migrating whales. It leans more upscale and polished than some other beach destinations, and it suits families who want a refined resort stay with excursions on the side.

Best for

  • Families who prefer upscale resorts
  • Whale watching in winter
  • Active families who like boats, fishing and desert adventures
  • Multigenerational trips with a mix of relaxing and exploring
  • Shorter trips with quick flights from the western U.S.

Los Cabos is a desert, so rain is rare for most of the year, and the winter and spring months are warm and sunny. Whale watching is typically best from about December through April. Summer is hot, and the Pacific hurricane season runs roughly May through November, with September and October the riskiest. Water temperatures are cooler in winter than in the Caribbean.

Getting There & Around

Making the journey simple.

By air

Los Cabos International (SJD) is near San José del Cabo and has nonstop flights from many U.S. cities, including routes from the Western and Southwestern U.S.

Transfers

About 15 to 25 minutes to San José del Cabo, and roughly 30 to 50 minutes to Cabo San Lucas and most Corridor resorts, depending on traffic.

Getting around

Most families use resort shuttles, private drivers and tour operators. Taxis are common in town areas, but fares should be agreed in advance.

Water taxis

Boats leave from Cabo San Lucas marina to see the Arch and nearby beaches. We confirm conditions and safety rules when we book.

Where to Stay

Choosing the right area.

Cabo San Lucas

The energetic hub with the marina, restaurants, shops and nightlife. Good for families with teens and those who want lots nearby.

The Tourist Corridor

Upscale resorts on coves and beaches between Cabo and San José, many with calmer swimming areas. A strong choice for families who want a resort-focused stay.

San José del Cabo

A charming, more relaxed town with an art district and a gentler pace. Great for families who prefer quiet and a local feel.

East Cape & Todos Santos

Quieter, more rustic corners reachable by road for travelers who want something different.

Good to Know

Practical details before you go.

Swimming safety

Many Pacific-facing beaches in Los Cabos have strong currents and rough surf and are not safe for swimming. Look for protected bays and resort areas with calm water, and always heed posted flags.

Entry

U.S. and Canadian citizens need a valid passport and a Mexican tourist permit. Requirements can change, and we confirm them at booking.

Money

Pesos are used locally, and USD is widely accepted in tourist areas.

Water & food

Drink bottled or purified water.

Sun & heat

The desert sun is strong. Pack sun protection, hats and plenty of water, especially in summer.

Safety

Use licensed transport, and check current U.S. and Canadian travel advisories before you go.

Planning Tips

How we'd plan it.

Advisor tips

  • Match your resort to the beach. Ask us which resorts sit on swimmable water.
  • Book whale watching and boat tours early if you are traveling in winter.
  • Winter evenings can be cool, so pack a light jacket.
  • Consider flexible travel insurance if you travel in September or October.

Questions

Common questions.

Can kids swim at the beach in Los Cabos?

Some beaches and resort coves are calm and safe, while many Pacific-side beaches are not. We choose resorts and excursions with that in mind.

When can we see whales?

Generally from about December through April, with the exact window varying by year.

Is Los Cabos too upscale for kids?

Not at all. Many resorts have family programs, and the destination is relaxed. It simply tends to feel more refined than some alternatives.

How long should we stay?

Five to seven nights is a popular length, which fits a beach day rhythm and a few excursions.

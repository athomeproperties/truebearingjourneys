Overview

Why families choose Aruba.

Aruba is a small, dry island in the southern Caribbean with famously steady sunshine and constant trade winds. It sits south of the main hurricane belt, which makes it a popular option for travelers who want to plan a Caribbean trip in summer or fall with less storm worry than many other islands (storms can still affect the region, so travel protection remains wise).

English is widely spoken, services are well developed, and most resorts are only minutes from the airport. Families enjoy the easy logistics, calm beaches on the leeward side and the mix of beach time with desert scenery and snorkeling.

Best for

  • First-time Caribbean travelers
  • Families who want easy logistics and short transfers
  • Summer and fall travel
  • Multigenerational trips
  • Travelers who want sun with a relaxed pace

Aruba is warm and sunny nearly all year, with steady breezes that keep the heat comfortable. The wettest period is typically October through January, though showers are usually brief. The island is generally considered outside the main hurricane belt, but no destination is entirely risk-free, so trip protection is still a good idea. The trade winds can make the east and north coasts rough, while the west and southwest beaches are calmer.

Getting There & Around

Making the journey simple.

By air

Queen Beatrix International (AUA) has nonstop flights from many U.S. cities and some Canadian cities. Aruba has U.S. preclearance, which means customs and immigration for U.S.-bound travelers are completed before departing. We confirm the details for your travel.

Transfers

Most resorts are about 10 to 20 minutes from the airport.

Getting around

Taxis, public buses and rental cars are all common. A 4x4 or guided tour is best for rugged areas such as Arikok National Park.

Water

Many families add a snorkel, catamaran or boat tour for a day on the water.

Where to Stay

Choosing the right area.

Palm Beach

Lively high-rise resort strip with shops, restaurants and water sports. Good for families who want action close by.

Eagle Beach

Wide, quieter beach with low-rise resorts and the famous divi-divi trees. Great for relaxed family time.

Oranjestad

The colorful capital with Dutch colonial architecture, shopping and cruise port.

Southeast Aruba

Local beaches such as Baby Beach, known for shallow, calm water good for young children.

Good to Know

Practical details before you go.

Entry

U.S. and Canadian citizens need a valid passport. Aruba also requires an online Embarkation/Disembarkation card to be completed before arrival, and we confirm the current steps at booking.

Money

USD is widely accepted, along with the Aruban florin.

Weather

Expect strong sun and steady wind. Reapply sunscreen often.

Water

Tap water is generally potable, but many visitors prefer bottled water.

Safety

Aruba is generally regarded as a comfortable and well-developed destination. Use normal travel precautions and check current advisories.

Insurance

Consider trip protection for any Caribbean travel between June and November.

Planning Tips

How we'd plan it.

Advisor tips

  • Choose Palm Beach for energy, Eagle Beach for calm.
  • Book a guided Arikok tour rather than driving rough roads on your own.
  • Wear a hat and bring a wind-proof layer for boat days.
  • Ask us which beaches are best for your children's ages.

Questions

Common questions.

Is Aruba good for kids?

Yes. Calm leeward beaches, English-speaking staff and short transfers make it an easy first Caribbean trip.

Is Aruba outside the hurricane belt?

It lies south of the main belt and is rarely hit directly, though storms can still affect the region.

Which beach is better, Eagle or Palm?

Palm Beach is livelier with more activities. Eagle Beach is wider and quieter.

How long should we stay?

A week is a comfortable length for beach days and a few outings.

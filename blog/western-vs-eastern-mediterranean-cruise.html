Western or Eastern Mediterranean Cruise? How to Choose

Barcelona, Rome and the French Riviera or Athens, Santorini and Dubrovnik? A guide to the two main Mediterranean cruise styles.

Photo: Jonathan Gallegos / Unsplash

Most Mediterranean cruise itineraries fall into two broad groups, western and eastern, and the right one depends on what you want to see. There are also itineraries that blend the two. Here is a simple way to compare them.

Western Mediterranean

Western itineraries typically sail between ports in Spain, France and Italy. Departures from Barcelona, Rome (through the port of Civitavecchia) or other Italian cities are common.

  • What you might see: Barcelona's architecture, the French Riviera, Florence and Pisa from the Livorno port, Rome, Naples, and the Amalfi Coast area.
  • Why families like it: familiar food, lots of famous cities, and a rich mix of art, history and beaches.
  • Keep in mind: big cities are crowded in peak summer and can involve long days of walking. Some ports are a significant distance from the main sights, which affects excursion time.

Eastern Mediterranean

Eastern itineraries focus on Greece, the Greek islands, Croatia and sometimes Turkey and beyond, often departing from Athens (Piraeus), Venice, or Rome.

  • What you might see: the Acropolis in Athens, Santorini and Mykonos, Dubrovnik's walled old town, and ancient sites.
  • Why families like it: islands, ancient history and scenery that feels different from a typical city break.
  • Keep in mind: Santorini and some islands are reached by tender (a small boat from the ship) and involve steps or a cable car. Check mobility needs and children's stamina.

Mixed itineraries

Some sailings combine both areas, with stops in Italy and Greece. These can be a good compromise if you cannot choose, though they may involve longer travel days at sea.

When to go

The main season is spring through fall. July and August are hot and busy, while late spring and early fall are often more comfortable and less crowded. Winter sailings exist but have fewer options, and some seasonal attractions close.

How long to sail

Seven nights is the most common length. Longer sailings let you see more, but may be tiring for young children.

Before and after the sailing

Many travelers add a hotel night or two in the departure city. Flights from North America often arrive in the morning, and an extra night helps with jet lag and reduces the risk of missing the ship if a flight is delayed.

Entry requirements

Europe has been updating its entry systems for non-European visitors, and rules can change. We confirm current requirements for your travel dates and your citizenship when you book.

Our rule of thumb

For first-time visitors who want famous cities, start in the west. For islands and ancient history, choose the east. See our Mediterranean cruise guide and our post on Mediterranean cruises with teens.

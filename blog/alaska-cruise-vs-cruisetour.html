Alaska Cruise or Cruisetour: Which One Fits Your Family?

A cruise-only Alaska trip is simple. A cruisetour adds days on land, including Denali. Here is how to decide which suits your family.

Photo: Peter Hansen / Unsplash

There are two main ways to see Alaska by ship. You can take a cruise only, or you can add several days on land before or after the sailing, which is usually called a cruisetour (or land-and-sea package). Both are good. They suit different families.

The cruise-only option

Most Alaska cruises last about seven days. Many sail the Inside Passage round trip from Seattle or Vancouver, visiting ports like Juneau, Skagway and Ketchikan, with scenic cruising past glaciers. One-way itineraries between Vancouver and ports like Seward or Whittier, near Anchorage, are also common.

  • Why choose it: simple logistics, a single hotel room that moves with you, and a fixed cost that includes meals and entertainment.
  • Trade-off: you see the coast and port towns, but not the interior of Alaska.

The cruisetour option

A cruisetour pairs the sailing with a few days inland. The classic add-on is Denali National Park, with its huge wilderness, wildlife viewing and views of North America's tallest peak on a clear day. Other stops can include Fairbanks or the Kenai Peninsula, and travel between them is usually by train or motorcoach.

  • Why choose it: more of the state, more wildlife and a deeper sense of Alaska's scale.
  • Trade-off: more moving parts, more nights in hotels and lodges, and a longer, usually more expensive trip.

What matters for kids

School-age and older children often love the land portion, especially train rides and wildlife tours. Younger children can find long motorcoach days tiring. If your kids are small, a cruise-only trip, or a short land extension at one location, may be easier.

Timing and booking

Land packages depend on lodge availability and season, so they are worth booking early. They typically run during the same summer window as the cruise season. We handle the coordination so flights, transfers and sailing dates line up.

A note on one-way versus round trip

One-way itineraries can save time you would otherwise spend sailing back, but they require your flights to start and end in different cities. Round-trip sailings from a single port are usually simpler, particularly for families driving to the departure city.

So which should you choose?

Choose a cruise-only trip if you want a relaxing, straightforward vacation or you have limited time. Choose a cruisetour if you want to see Denali and the interior, have older kids or a multigenerational group, and can spend about two weeks.

Mayan Ruins and Cenotes With Kids: What to Know Before You Go

Chichen Itza, Tulum, Coba and swimming in cenotes: practical tips for visiting the Riviera Maya's top sights with children.

Photo: Héctor Mavare / Unsplash

A beach week in the Riviera Maya can include much more than sand. The region is full of ancient Mayan sites and natural swimming holes called cenotes, and both can be highlights for kids who like adventure. A few details make these outings easier.

Choosing a ruin

  • Chichen Itza: the most famous site, with its great pyramid. It is a long day trip from the main resort areas, so it suits older children and groups happy to spend several hours on the road.
  • Tulum: smaller, closer to the coast, and set on a cliff above the sea. It is easier for younger kids and pairs well with a beach stop.
  • Coba: a jungle site where you can rent bikes or take a pedicab between structures. It appeals to active families.

Most large sites have rules about climbing structures, and in many cases you cannot climb the pyramids. Preservation is a priority, so expect some restricted areas.

Beat the heat

The sun and humidity are strong. Go early in the morning, bring water, hats and sunscreen, and wear sturdy, comfortable shoes. Ruins often have little shade, so plan for breaks.

Cenote basics

Cenotes are sinkholes filled with clear freshwater. Some are open-air, others are caves with light shafts, and some have platforms for jumping. Water is cool, which feels great after a hot morning.

  • Many cenotes require a shower before entering and ask visitors to avoid regular sunscreen and insect repellent, because chemicals harm the water. Look for biodegradable products, or use a rash guard.
  • Life jackets are often available and are a good idea for children and weaker swimmers.
  • Cave cenotes can feel dark and enclosed. Choose open-air ones if anyone is nervous.

Go with a reputable guide

Guided tours handle transportation, entry fees and local knowledge, and they can combine a ruin and a cenote in one day. We work with trusted providers rather than choosing the cheapest option.

Pacing with children

One main outing per day is plenty. Younger kids may be happiest with a cenote swim and a snack, while older kids can handle a full ruin day. Build in rest at the resort pool.

Water and food

Stick to bottled or purified water, and ask your resort or guide about safe options for snacks and meals during excursions.

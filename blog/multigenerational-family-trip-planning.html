Planning a Multigenerational Trip: Grandparents, Parents and Kids Together

How to choose a destination, pace and lodging that work for three generations, and keep the planning from falling on one person.

A trip with grandparents, parents and children is one of the most meaningful ways to travel, and one of the hardest to plan. Everyone has different energy levels, budgets and ideas of fun. A few principles help.

Start with a shared goal

Before you look at destinations, talk about what each family wants. A reunion, a milestone birthday and a simple beach week call for different plans. Having a goal makes later decisions easier.

Pick a destination with something for every age

Look for places where a range of activities is close at hand. - Cruises let each person choose their own day while still meeting for dinner. - All-inclusive resorts offer kids' programs, easy beaches and low-effort dining. - Hawaii and Orlando have a mix of gentle and active options.

Pace matters most

Plan fewer activities than you think you need. Build in rest time, especially for older travelers and small children. Not everyone has to do everything together. Sometimes the best moments are shared meals after separate adventures.

Think about accessibility

If anyone has limited mobility, choose destinations and rooms that suit them. Ask about walking distances, stairs, accessible rooms and transport. Port stops that involve tenders or steep hills may not be suitable for everyone.

Rooms and layout

Consider whether you want connecting rooms, adjoining rooms, or a villa with shared space. Some groups love being together in one space, others prefer separate rooms to give everyone privacy.

Budget conversations

Money can be sensitive. Decide up front who is paying for what, and be open about price ranges. Some families split the cost of shared items such as the villa or the cruise fare and pay for their own extras.

Dates and school calendars

Syncing calendars for several households can be the hardest part. Start early, and find the dates that work for most before you look at prices. The more flexible you are, the more choices you have.

Document and health checks

Make sure everyone's passports are valid and that each traveler has what they need. Travelers with health conditions may want to consider travel insurance and speak with their doctor before the trip.

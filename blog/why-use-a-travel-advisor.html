Why Use a Travel Advisor When You Can Book Online?

What an independent travel advisor does, how advisors are paid, and when it makes sense to have one for a family trip.

You can book almost any trip yourself online. So why would a family use a travel advisor? The honest answer is that it depends on the trip. Here is when having a person on your side helps, and what to ask any advisor you consider.

What an advisor does

A good advisor starts by listening: your dates, budget, kids' ages and what matters to you. Then they recommend options that fit, rather than handing you a list of search results. They handle booking, keep track of payment deadlines and documents, and are available if something changes.

When it helps most

  • Complex trips: cruises, multi-stop itineraries, group or multigenerational travel.
  • Unfamiliar destinations: where you do not know which area or resort suits your family.
  • Trips with a lot at stake: big milestones or large budgets, where a mistake would be costly.
  • When plans change: having someone to call to sort out changes, delays or cancellations.

When you may not need one

If you are booking a simple trip to a place you know well, you may be perfectly happy doing it yourself. An advisor adds the most value when decisions are complicated or time is short.

How advisors are paid

Practices vary. Many advisors are paid commission by the travel suppliers they book, such as cruise lines, resorts and tour companies. Some also charge planning fees for certain services. It is reasonable to ask any advisor how they are paid and whether there is a fee, before you start.

Questions to ask

  • How are you compensated, and are there any fees?
  • Which suppliers do you work with, and why?
  • What happens if something goes wrong while I am traveling?
  • How will we communicate during the planning and the trip?

Independent does not mean alone

Independent advisors often work through a host agency that provides supplier relationships, training and support. This helps individual advisors access established suppliers and resources.

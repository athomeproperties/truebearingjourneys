Travel Insurance for Families: What It Covers and When to Buy

A plain explanation of trip cancellation, medical coverage and timing, and what to ask before you buy a policy.

Travel insurance is one of those things many people skip until they need it. For family trips, which tend to involve more people, more money upfront and more chances for something to change, it deserves a look. This is general information, not insurance advice. Read the policy you are considering carefully.

What it can cover

  • Trip cancellation: reimburses prepaid, non-refundable costs if you cancel for a covered reason, such as illness or injury.
  • Trip interruption: helps if you must cut the trip short for a covered reason.
  • Travel delay and baggage: helps with extra costs from delays or lost luggage.
  • Medical care and evacuation: many health insurance plans do not cover care abroad, or cover it only in a limited way. Emergency medical coverage and medical evacuation can matter a lot, particularly on a cruise or in a remote place.

What it usually does not cover

Many policies do not cover reasons that are not on a defined list. Fear of travel, a change of mind or ordinary work conflicts are usually excluded. Some policies offer an upgrade that allows you to cancel for any reason and receive part of your money back, usually at a higher cost.

Timing can matter

Some benefits, such as a waiver for pre-existing medical conditions or the option to cancel for any reason, often require you to buy the policy within a limited window after your first trip deposit. Ask about deadlines before you book, so you do not lose options.

Questions to ask

  • What is the coverage limit for medical care and evacuation?
  • Does it cover cancellation due to hurricanes or other weather?
  • How are pre-existing conditions handled?
  • Is coverage included for all travelers, including children?
  • What is the process for filing a claim?

Supplier protections are different

Airlines, cruise lines and resorts each have their own cancellation terms, which vary. These are not the same as insurance and often provide credit or partial refunds only. Read them carefully.

Credit card benefits

Some credit cards include travel protections. They tend to have lower limits and conditions, so compare them with a stand-alone policy.

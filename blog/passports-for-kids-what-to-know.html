Passports for Kids: What Parents Need to Know Before Booking

How children's passports work for U.S. citizens: who can apply, how long they last and why to start early.

If your family is heading abroad, the first thing to check is passports. Children's passports have different rules from adults', and delays can derail a trip. This is general information for U.S. citizens. Always confirm current details with the U.S. Department of State.

Do kids need a passport?

For international air travel, yes. Everyone, including infants, needs their own passport. Some cruises that begin and end in the same U.S. port have more flexible rules for U.S. citizens, but a passport is strongly recommended, because it makes emergencies much easier to handle if you need to fly home from a foreign port.

How the process differs for children

  • Children under 16 generally must apply in person, and typically both parents or guardians need to give consent. If one parent cannot attend, there is a consent form process, so ask early.
  • Passports for children under 16 are generally valid for five years, while adult passports are valid for ten years.
  • A child's passport cannot usually be renewed by mail, so plan for a new application at each renewal.

Start early

Processing times change, and busy seasons can be longer. Check the State Department's current estimates, and apply well before your trip. Faster options exist for an extra fee, but they are not a substitute for planning ahead.

Check the rules for your destination

Many countries require that a passport be valid for a certain period after your travel dates. Check the requirement for each destination on your itinerary, including any connecting countries. We confirm entry requirements for your trip at booking, since they can change.

Names must match

The name on your passport should match the name on your flight tickets and cruise booking exactly. Tell us immediately if a name has changed or contains special characters.

Traveling with only one parent or with grandparents

Some countries and airlines ask for a letter of consent when a child travels without both parents. Carrying a signed, notarized letter and copies of birth certificates is a good practice. Requirements vary, so ask us what applies to your trip.

Other documents to consider

Depending on destination, you may need a visa, an electronic travel authorization, or an arrival form. We check what is needed for each traveler.

Keep copies

Take photos of passports and store a copy separately, such as in a secure cloud folder, in case of loss.

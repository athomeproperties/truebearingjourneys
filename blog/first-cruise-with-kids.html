Your First Cruise With Kids: What to Know Before You Book

Cabins, kids' clubs, ports, minimum ages and what is and is not included. A first-timer's guide to cruising with children.

A cruise can be one of the easiest ways to travel with children. You unpack once, meals are covered, and there is something going on all day. A few decisions at the start make the difference between a smooth trip and a stressful one.

Choose the right ship

Cruise ships vary a lot. Large ships have water slides, kids' clubs, theaters and many dining options. Smaller ships are quieter and visit smaller ports, but usually have fewer children's activities. For families, larger ships with strong kids' programs are often the easiest match, though preferences vary.

Check minimum ages

Cruise lines set their own rules. Many require babies to be at least six months old at the start of a sailing, and some itineraries, such as those with several days at sea in a row, require a longer minimum age. Children's clubs have age groups and their own rules about toilet training. We confirm these before you book.

Think about the cabin

  • Inside cabins are the most affordable and are dark, which helps with naps.
  • Oceanview cabins have a window.
  • Balcony cabins give private outdoor space, though supervising small children needs care.
  • Family cabins and suites sleep more people. Connecting cabins are useful for bigger groups or teens.

What is included

Typically, meals in the main dining areas, entertainment and many activities are included. Extras often include specialty restaurants, drinks, Wi-Fi, spa services, gratuities and shore excursions. Looking at the total cost, not just the base fare, helps you compare fairly.

Shore days

Plan about one activity per port rather than trying to do everything. Young children usually enjoy a beach day or a simple, short excursion more than a long tour. Always allow plenty of time to be back at the ship before departure.

Seasickness

Most modern ships are stable, and many travelers feel little movement. If you or your kids are prone to motion sickness, a cabin in the middle of the ship on a lower deck usually feels the least motion. Ask your doctor or pharmacist about remedies.

Documents

For U.S. citizens, some cruises that start and end in the same U.S. port have more flexible document rules, but a passport is strongly recommended and is required for many itineraries. We confirm what applies for your sailing and your citizenship. Passports for children have their own rules, covered in our post on children's passports.

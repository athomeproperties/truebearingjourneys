Orlando With Young Kids: Practical Tips That Save Your Trip

Heat, naps, lines and strollers: realistic tips for a theme-park vacation with toddlers and preschoolers.

Photo: Mick Haupt / Unsplash

A theme-park vacation with small children can be magical. It can also turn into a long, hot day of meltdowns if it is planned like an adult trip. These tips help keep it enjoyable.

Go slower than you think

Plan about half of what you would do on your own. One or two main attractions in the morning, a midday break, then something gentle in the evening is a better rhythm than racing from ride to ride. Young children tire quickly, and a rested child is a happier one.

Plan around naps and heat

Central Florida is hot and humid for much of the year, with afternoon thunderstorms common in summer. Arrive early, when it is cooler and lines are shorter, and head back to the hotel for a nap or pool time in the afternoon. Return in the evening if everyone has energy.

Stay close if you can

Staying near the parks, or at a resort with easy transportation, makes midday breaks realistic. Long commutes work against you when children need rest. We can compare on-site and nearby hotels based on your budget.

Know the height requirements

Many rides have minimum heights, and young children can be disappointed. Check requirements before your trip and prepare your kids. Both resorts have plenty of attractions without height limits.

Strollers and carriers

Even children who no longer use a stroller at home often tire out in a park. Bring a stroller or consider renting one. Strollers can also hold bags, jackets and snacks.

Snacks, water and sun protection

  • Carry water bottles and refill them often.
  • Bring snacks that suit picky eaters.
  • Use sunscreen, hats and sunglasses, and reapply.
  • Pack a light poncho for sudden storms.

Dining and special needs

Popular restaurants, character meals and shows may need advance reservations. If anyone has food allergies or mobility needs, tell us early so we can plan around them and check each park's current accessibility services.

Make room for non-park time

A hotel pool day, a mini-golf outing or a quiet evening can be a highlight for small children. Not every day needs to be a park day.

Booking a Trip During Hurricane Season: How to Plan Smart

The Atlantic hurricane season runs June to November. How to weigh the risk, choose destinations and protect your trip.

The Atlantic hurricane season officially runs from June 1 through November 30. That does not mean every trip during those months is risky, but it does mean planning with a bit more care, especially for the Caribbean, Mexico's Caribbean coast, Florida and the Gulf.

Know when risk is highest

Activity tends to build through the summer and is generally most active from mid-August through October. Early June and late November are usually quieter, though storms can still happen.

Think about the destination

Some places are more exposed than others. Islands in the central and northern Caribbean see more storms than the far southern Caribbean. Aruba, for example, lies south of the typical storm track, which is why many people look at it for late-summer travel. Hawaii and Alaska have different weather considerations entirely.

Consider your flexibility

If your dates are flexible, you can sometimes avoid the peak weeks. If you must travel during school holidays or specific dates, weigh the risk with your tolerance for change.

Understand cancellation and change policies

Some resorts and cruise lines offer flexible or hurricane-related change policies, though terms differ. Read them before you pay, and ask us to explain how your supplier handles a named storm.

Consider travel insurance

Some policies cover trip cancellation or interruption due to a named storm, but the conditions and timing vary. Read the details, and buy early. See our post on travel insurance for families.

Choose a resort with plenty to do

If the weather turns, a resort with indoor activities, a spa or entertainment helps keep kids occupied. Ask about backup plans.

Watch the forecast, not the headlines

Check official forecasts from national weather services as your trip approaches. Contact your advisor if a storm appears, so we can help you understand your options and contact suppliers.

Cruises and storms

Cruise lines may change itineraries to avoid storms. This is usually done for safety and may mean different ports than planned. Be flexible.

The upside

Fewer crowds and often lower prices can make hurricane season appealing. Many people travel in these months without a problem, and planning properly gives you peace of mind.
